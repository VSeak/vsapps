# FIRE

The owner's household money tracker: salary history for them and their partner, investment accounts updated by hand, and how that compares with retiring at a normal age, a FIRE age and the S&P 500. One user: only the owner's login gets in (`fire_owner`). The partner is a person in the data, not a user. All money is pre-tax for now (the user chose this; no Roth vs pre-tax split yet).

## How it's built

- Same shape as Top Out: `index.html` (header and shell), `styles.css`, plain JS in `js/` loaded as classic `<script>` tags that share one global scope, in the order listed in `index.html`; `boot()` runs last. Don't declare the same top-level name in two files. No build step. supabase-js pinned from jsDelivr.
  - `core.js`: `CONFIG`, helpers copied from Top Out (`esc`, `flash`, `ask`, `busy`, `crumbs`, field errors, `markRequired`, `logError`, dates, install banner), money (`money`, `moneyShort`, `pct`, `num`), `sparkline`.
  - `app.js`: `boot`, `loadMe`, `route`, the leave check and Unsaved marks, sign-in pages.
  - `overview.js`: Home (`homePage`, the Overview: Invested with Times Your Pay, the FIRE card, Milestones, tiles) and `#/spending`. The FIRE card: FIRE number = latest year's spending in today's dollars ÷ withdrawal rate, % of the way, FIRE date and each person's age then, the balance at the first person's retirement age, Coast FIRE (what grows to the FIRE number by then with no more saving; `coastNeed` rises toward the FIRE number as retirement nears, `coastHit` is the first month the projection reaches it, shown as "there in <year>"), a chart (invested at each update date, then projected, the FIRE number as a line, the Coast FIRE amount as a green dotted curve to retirement with a dot and "Coast <year>" where the projection meets it; hover or drag for a line with the year and amount, `chartHover`) and a Save More a Year slider (0–$60k; only `#fiBody` redraws so a drag isn't cut off). Savings = what you and employers put in over the last 12 months (`putInBetween`). `project` steps month by month at the real return. Milestones (`MILESTONES`, every 5 years, the user asked): 1× pay at 30, 2× at 35, 3× at 40, 4× at 45, 6× at 50, 7× at 55, 8× at 60, 9× at 65 (halfway, not part of the rule), 10× at 67, by the first person's age, against household pay (that year's for a reached age, today's ahead). Projected and Actual columns (the user chose saved projections): opening the Overview saves today's projection for every age still ahead to `fire_projections` (one row per age per day, upsert); a reached age shows the last projection saved before that birthday ("as of") beside the actual balance then (`totalOn`) and the difference. Ages reached before saving began show only Actual.
  - `accounts.js`: `#/accounts` (grouped by owner, then Closed) and `#/account/<id>` (balance chart, totals, updates).
  - `salary.js`: `#/salary` (Household: each year's pay for both, total, You Saved and Savings Rate; then a card per person: Pay Rate Now, Raises a Year, After Inflation, Lifetime Pay, jobs, By Year) and `#/job/<id>` (Pay Changes, Bonuses, By Year with W-2 totals). The math is plain functions: `payPeriods`, `rateOn`, `jobYear`, `personYears`, `rateGrowth`, `savedByYear`; dates are day numbers (`dnum`, UTC) so there are no time-zone surprises.
  - `contrib.js`: contribution plans (the user asked, to make the projection more accurate): the Contributions card and dialog on an account page (`contribCardHTML`, `contribDialog`), `contribYearly` (a line's yearly dollars on a day), `planBetween`, `planYearlyNow`. The card shows each line's yearly rate and, when it differs (a mid-year start or stop), what it puts in this calendar year counting only the days it runs (the user read the yearly rate as this year's total). Uses: a new update's You Put In / Employer Put In start as what the plan says went in since the update before (and follow the date until typed in); the FIRE card saves at the plan's yearly total at today's pay when there is any plan (last 12 months shown beside it), else the last 12 months; Salary's You Saved uses the plan for days an account's updates don't cover.
  - `sp500.js`: `SP500`, the S&P 500 total return index (dividends reinvested, Yahoo Finance `^SP500TR`), one month-end close a month from January 1988 to `end`. Built once by hand (the user chose a file in the repo over a live data service); the file's comment has the address to fetch newer months. Checked against the published yearly total returns (2008 −37.00%, 2019 +31.49%, 2025 +17.88%).
  - `compare.js`: `#/sp500` (Vs. the S&P 500: All Accounts with a chart, then By Account; linked from Accounts and an Overview tile) and the Vs. the S&P 500 card on each account page (`accountCompareHTML`). `shadowOf(account)`: the first update's whole balance buys the index on that day; each later update's money (you, employer, moved) is spread over the days since the update before and buys month by month at the index on each slice's middle day (`spOn` interpolates between month-ends); moved-out money sells. Rates are money-weighted (`xirr`), shown only with a year or more of history. Accounts starting before 1988 are left out. Dates after the data's end use its last value, and the page says so. Sanity check: an account that exactly tracks the index shows $0 difference and the index's own rate.
  - `settings.js`: `#/settings`: People (names, birth dates) and Assumptions (with Reset to Defaults).
- **Same Supabase project as Sit Start and Top Out** (same URL and key). Tables are all `fire_*`; `supabase/schema.sql` creates them. Every table's rule is `fire_is_owner()`: the signed-in email is in `fire_owner` (one row, set in the SQL Editor). Any other login signs in and sees "No Access". There is no sign-up and no invite: the owner uses the login they already have for the other apps.
- `login_in_other_app()` is replaced here to also answer for `fire_owner`, so Sit Start never deletes that login. If Top Out's schema is ever re-run, re-run that function from this schema too (it must check both `team_staff` and `fire_owner`).
- Errors go to the shared `app_errors` with `app = 'fire'` (the schema widens its check).

## Data

- `fire_people(id, name, birth_date, position)`: the two people (seeded as You and Partner; renamed in Settings). Birth dates turn dates into ages.
- `fire_settings`: one row of assumptions. A null column means the default in `DEFAULTS` (settings.js), so the defaults live in one place and Reset to Defaults sets every column to null (the user asked for a reset). Expected return is **after inflation** (real, default 7%), inflation 3%, withdrawal rate 4%, normal retirement age 65, salary history starts at age 22.
- `fire_accounts(owner_id, name, kind, institution, job_id, closed_on, notes, position)`: owner null = Joint. `kind` is a key of `KINDS` (accounts.js). `job_id` links an employer plan to its job (From Job in the account dialog). Closed accounts keep their history and list under Closed.
- `fire_updates(account_id, as_of, balance, contributed, employer, moved)`: an update any time (the user asked; not just yearly). `contributed` = what you put in since the last update, `employer` = match/profit sharing since the last update, `moved` = money moved in from another of your accounts (a rollover; minus for out), which is not new savings. Growth = balance − everything put in − moved. One update per account per day.
- `fire_jobs(person_id, employer, title, start_date, end_date, paychecks, pay_days, pay_anchor)`: a person can have several jobs at once (the user has two). Add Job also takes the starting pay. **Pay days** (the user asked: pay and contributions come in on payday, e.g. the 7th and 22nd): `pay_days` = day(s) of the month for twice a month (two) or monthly (one); `pay_anchor` = any one payday for every two weeks or weekly. With pay days (`hasPayDays`), a year's pay is the paychecks paid that year (`paychecks()`, as a W-2 counts it): each pays the days since the payday before at 1/paychecks of the yearly rate, prorated for a partial first or last one, mixed across a raise; a weekend payday is paid the Friday before; the last paycheck after the job's end still counts. Percent and per-paycheck contributions go in on those paydays too. Without pay days, everything is spread by day as before. Migration `2026-10-15-pay-days.sql`.
- `fire_pay(job_id, start_date, kind, amount, hours_per_week)`: pay changes (the user gets raises mid-year). `kind` salary (amount per year), hourly (amount per hour × hours per week × 52) or none (a gap). Each rate lasts until the job's next pay change or its end date. A year's estimate is each rate prorated by days, plus bonuses; the current year is estimated to Dec 31.
- `fire_bonuses(job_id, paid_on, amount, percent)`: dollars, or a percent of the job's yearly rate on that date. The Bonuses table shows both ways (Of Pay and Amount; the one typed in is bright, the worked-out one muted; the user asked).
- `fire_job_actuals(job_id, year, amount)`: the W-2 total for that job and year (the hint points to box 5, since box 1 leaves out pre-tax 401(k) money). It replaces the estimate, bonuses included; years without one show Est. (the user's second job is ~6 hrs/week but sporadic).
- Salary numbers: Pay Rate = every job's yearly rate on Dec 31 (today for this year), no bonuses. Raises a Year = growth of that rate from the first year with one to now; After Inflation uses the Inflation setting (no CPI data yet). Today's $ = earned × (1 + inflation) ^ years ago. Years run from the start age (or the first job) to now, gaps shown. You Saved = `contributed` only (not employer or moved), each update's money spread evenly over the days since the update before.
- `fire_year_pay(person_id, year, amount)`: W-2 totals by year for years without job details (the user doesn't have old pay rates). A person's (+ W-2 Total on their card, or tap a year in their By Year) replaces their jobs' numbers that year; a joint one (`person_id` null; + Joint W-2 Total, or tap a Household year; they married in 2025 and file jointly) replaces the household Total that year while each person's column stays. `householdYears()` (salary.js) builds the household rows and `payByYear` that Salary, Spending (Of Pay) and the Overview (Times Your Pay, milestones) all use. Without job rates, the Overview's "pay now" is the latest year's household total. Raises a Year falls back to growth of yearly pay between finished years (`earnedGrowth`) when there are no pay rates. Migration `2026-10-15-year-pay.sql`.
- `fire_contributions(account_id, source, kind, percent, amount, per, job_id, start_date, end_date, note)`: `source` you / employer; `kind` percent (of the job's yearly rate that day, bonuses not counted) or amount `per` year / month / paycheck (the job's `paychecks`). A line counts between its dates and **never while its job isn't current**: a job's end date stops every line tied to it (the user asked), even with no Stopped date. To change 4% to 6%, set Stopped on the old line and add a new one. `fire_jobs.paychecks` 12 / 24 / 26 / 52 (default 24, twice a month). Migration `supabase/migrations/2026-10-15-contributions.sql`.
- `fire_spending(year, amount)`: one number per year (the user agreed). The Spending page shows it in today's dollars, the change from the year before, and as a share of household pay.
- Possible next steps (not asked for yet): Roth vs pre-tax, CPI data instead of the flat Inflation setting.

## Conventions

- Look: "Ink & Ember": warm paper page, deep ink, ember-orange accent; Outfit headings, IBM Plex Sans body, tabular numbers for money. Tokens on `:root` with dark mode. Logo: a flame (`icon.svg`, inline in the header and on the root home card).
- Works at ~400px wide. Title case for buttons, headings and labels; sentence case for hints and messages.
- Dialogs use `ask()` like Top Out. Save buttons (`.primary`) grey until a field changes.
- Projections are labeled as assumptions, not advice.
